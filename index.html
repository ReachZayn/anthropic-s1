Analysis by Sameer · personal views · not investment advice
1 · The loss

$81bn of the $80.7bn loss is marks, not operations.

Anthropic booked a $80.748bn GAAP net loss for the first half of 2026. The operating business lost $693m over the same six months. The gap is two non-cash marks: $72.388bn on convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs, and $8.720bn on other financial instruments carried at fair value, both driven by the company's own valuation rising.

No cash left for either mark. The cost arrives later, as dilution when the instruments convert into shares.

2 · What the loss turns into

$10.2bn of notes is carried at $108.6bn. That becomes shares.

Since November 2023 Anthropic has issued $10.2bn of convertible promissory notes. At 30 June 2026 they sit on the balance sheet at a fair value of $108.6bn, because they convert into equity at prices far below today's valuation. The $72.4bn mark is the growth in that gap.

Redeemable convertible preferred stock is carried at $157.5bn against a $136.3bn liquidation preference. Amazon's notes convert only up to a 12% ownership cap; anything above rolls over as a new note.

The number that matters, the post-conversion share count, is still blank in the preliminary filing.

3 · The business

Revenue outran compute, and the operating line turned.

Quarterly revenue went from $0.79bn in Q2 2025 to $11.56bn in Q2 2026, about 15 times in a year and 2.4 times in the last quarter alone. Compute and infrastructure expense fell from 177% of revenue to 48%. The filing credits part of that to pricing: Claude Opus 5.5 cut customer cost per task by about 40% against Opus 5.

Q2's $974m operating income is after $1.26bn of stock-based compensation. The filing's own adjusted measure, which strips SBC, non-cash charitable contributions and the $1.5bn Bartz settlement, shows $1.62bn for the half, a 10% adjusted margin against a (4)% GAAP one.

One caution the filing states itself: models released in the prior six months produced over 80% of revenue in the quarter to 30 September 2026. Revenue is tied to release cadence.

4 · Who's paying

Concentration is falling as the base broadens.

Customers paying $100,000 or more a year went from over 950 to over 11,900 in twelve months. The top five accounted for about 25% of revenue over nine months and none passed 10%. Revenue from outside the top five rose from 64% to 79% of the total between Q1 and Q3 2026, while each top-five customer still set a record.

Deferred revenue, cash billed ahead of service, rose from $269m to $1,650m in six months. Receivables rose faster, from $909m to $5.8bn.

The filing's Run-Rate Revenue figure, dated 31 October 2026, is left blank in this preliminary version.

5 · The cash

Rented now, leased later: why capex looks tiny.

Operating cash flow was +$3.9bn for the half against the −$80.7bn net loss, because both marks are added straight back. Purchases of property and equipment were only $482m, so cash after capex was about $3.4bn.

That flatters the picture twice. Compute is rented from cloud providers, so it runs through operating expense, and the Broadcom equipment leases bill only once hardware is "ready for service", so the capex-like cash arrives later as rent. Inside operating cash flow, $1.4bn of deferred revenue and $2.7bn of payables growth helped, while $5.0bn of receivables growth hurt.

The $110.4bn raised was preferred stock, not debt. Of it, $19bn was parked in short-term investments.

6 · The bill

The filing's own totals are larger than the named deals.

At 30 June 2026 the filing states $265.6bn of fixed or minimum guaranteed commitments under compute agreements, on terms of one to twenty years, and separately $321.4bn of leases signed but not yet commenced, up from $50.2bn six months earlier. Agreements entered into after 31 December 2025 alone carry $417bn of future minimum payments. These overlap each other and the named deals, so they are never added together.

The only year-by-year schedule in the filing is the non-cancelable commitments table at 31 December 2025, $54.6bn, which pre-dates most of the big agreements. The June 2026 table is blank. For scale, Q2 revenue times four is $46.2bn.

Three of the largest suppliers also stand behind $77bn of undrawn note facilities (Google $20bn, Amazon $15bn, Broadcom $42bn), beside a $15bn revolver. The map in the next section draws that loop.

8 · What's still open

What I'd want answered before underwriting it.

The filing shows demand that is broadening, a first quarter of operating profit, and a compute bill that is mostly still to come. It leaves the offer price, the final share count and the post-conversion ownership open.

  • Does the Q2 margin survive the leases commencing?$321bn of leases had not started billing at 30 June. Operating income was $974m in the quarter.
  • How many shares does $108.6bn of notes become?Principal is $10.2bn. The conversion count isn't in the preliminary filing.
  • How much of the revenue is cadence?Over 80% of Q3 revenue came from models under six months old. A slower release year is a different revenue year.
  • Is the supplier-lender-shareholder loop a moat or a dependency?Amazon, Google and Broadcom sell the compute, hold the note facilities and own or may own the shares.
  • What does the June 2026 commitments table say?It is blank. The Dec 2025 schedule totals $54.6bn; the June figures are five to eight times that.
